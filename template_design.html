How to Do the Hotel Lobby AI Trend with Seedance 2.5 The Hotel Lobby AI trend replaces Quavo and Takeoff in their viral “Hotel Lobby” performance with you, a friend, a celebrity, or almost any two characters. Instead of manually face-swapping the original footage, you can recreate the full 15-second performance with Seedance 2.5 in Kai, using the original movement as a reference while giving the AI completely new people to perform it. The workflow takes four steps: create a character sheet for each person, use a simplified motion-reference clip of the performance, generate the new version with Seedance 2.5, and add the original song back in Kapwing. What makes this method work particularly well is that the AI never needs the original performers' appearance as its reference. The motion clip is converted into a simple line drawing that preserves the gestures, head turns, mouth movements, timing, and camera framing while stripping away most of the visual information that could interfere with your new characters. A single portrait can work for basic image generation, but this performance includes head turns, side profiles, arm movements, and changes in body angle. Giving the model several consistent views of the same person makes it much easier for Seedance 2.5 to preserve character identity across the entire clip. Reference photos used to create AI character sheets for the Hotel Lobby AI trend. Start with clear photos of each person so the character sheets have a strong reference. Open Kai's Character Sheet Generator, attach one clear photo of the first person, and use an image prompt that generates: Front-facing portrait Three-quarter portrait Side profile Front full-body view Three-quarter full-body view Side full-body view Back full-body view You should also specify the outfit at this stage. The character sheet effectively becomes the visual source of truth for the final video, so it's more reliable to dress the person in the character sheet than to ask the model to invent an outfit later. Step 2: Download the Hotel Lobby Motion Reference The recognizable part of this trend comes from a specific section of Quavo and Takeoff's “Hotel Lobby” performance on A COLORS SHOW. Across the examples we reviewed, creators overwhelmingly use the approximately 15-second section beginning around 0:14.6 of the performance. It contains many of the gestures that make the clip instantly recognizable, including the forward point, forearm across the face, turn away, and shush gesture. For our workflow, that sequence has been converted into a line-drawing motion reference. The simplified clip preserves primarily: Body movement Hand gestures Head movement Mouth movement Timing Camera framing At the same time, it removes much of the performers' original facial detail, clothing, jewelry, and color information. The result is generally more controllable. ↓ Download video Hotel Lobby AI trend comparison showing the original performance, line-drawing motion reference, and AI recreation. The sketch keeps the original timing and gestures while our character sheets control who appears in the final video. Step 3: Recreate the Performance with Seedance 2.5 Open Kai and attach your three reference files in this order: Hotel Lobby motion clip Character sheet for the person who should appear on the right Character sheet for the person who should appear on the left The order matters because Kapwing maps the uploaded assets to the media references used inside your prompt. Next, select Seedance 2.5 under your Video Clips model preferences and use the following video prompt. With the motion clip and both character sheets attached, we gave Seedance 2.5 one prompt for the full performance. Kai will show you the planned generation and credit cost before it begins. Check that the plan contains one continuous video rather than several separate clips, then confirm the generation. When the video finishes, download it or open it directly in the Kapwing editor. Step 4: Add the Hotel Lobby Audio The generated Seedance clip does not include the original song, so the final step is adding the audio back. Open the Hotel Lobby AI Trend template in Kapwing. The correct section of the song is already positioned on the timeline. Right-click the placeholder video, select Replace, and choose your generated clip. Because the audio and motion reference begin on the same frame, the mouth movements and gestures should line up with the music without requiring you to manually search for the correct point in the song. Small visual artifacts are much easier to notice once the audio is playing, particularly around: Hands crossing the face Fast finger movements Profiles Jewelry Mouth shapes Hair around the face You can fix minor presentation issues in the editor, add text or captions, crop the frame, and then export the finished video. How to Recreate Other AI Performance Trends The same workflow isn't limited to “Hotel Lobby.” Once you've created strong character sheets, you can reuse them with other performance clips. The main things that change are: The motion-reference video The environment described in the prompt The audio added during editing For example, we used the same character sheets to recreate a section of Fetty Wap and Monty's “679” performance on On The Radar. Instead of creating entirely new characters, we attached: A 15-second line-drawing reference of the “679” performance The same right-hand character sheet The same left-hand character sheet AI recreation of the 679 performance using a line-drawing motion reference and reusable character sheets. The same setup works beyond Hotel Lobby — we reused the character sheets with a new motion reference for “679.” The character instructions stayed almost identical. The main prompt change was describing the green radio-studio environment and preserving the original camera push-in. That makes reusable character sheets particularly valuable if you plan to test several AI video trends. Once the identity reference is working, each new video becomes more of a motion-transfer problem than a character-generation problem. Best Practices for AI Video Swaps Use the Same Section of the Performance as the Trend For recreation trends, recognition often depends less on the song itself than on a particular visual moment. Using a different verse may technically be the same track, but viewers won't necessarily recognize the reference immediately. Match the section, timing, framing, and gestures that are already associated with the trend. Use a Character Sheet Instead of One Photo One portrait gives the AI information about one angle of the face. A character sheet gives it multiple angles plus the person's full body. That becomes much more important when the reference performance includes side profiles, head turns, crossed arms, or movement toward and away from the camera. Put the Final Outfit in the Character Sheet Don't rely on the video prompt alone for wardrobe consistency. If the person needs to wear a black hoodie, white sneakers, or a specific jacket throughout the generation, put those clothes directly into their character sheet. Then reinforce the same outfit in the video prompt. Separate Motion From Appearance The strongest part of this workflow is giving different references different jobs. The motion clip should communicate what happens. The character sheet should communicate who is doing it. The prompt should communicate where it happens and how it should look. When one reference is expected to control all three at once, unwanted visual details are much more likely to carry over. Decide Who Gets Which Side Before Generating The two performers do not have identical choreography. In the Hotel Lobby clip, one side receives several of the most recognizable gestures. In other performances, one performer may spend more time directly at the microphone or closer to the camera. Put the person you want to emphasize in the stronger role before generating rather than trying to swap positions afterward. Reuse Good Character Sheets A strong character sheet can work across multiple videos. Keep the same character sheet in your Kapwing media library and change only the motion clip and scene description.
